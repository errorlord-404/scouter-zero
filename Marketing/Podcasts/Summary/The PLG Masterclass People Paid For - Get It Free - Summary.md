## Overview and Context

Product-led growth (PLG) as a term was coined in **2016** by Blake Bartlett. Google search interest began rising noticeably around **2017**, peaked around **2023**, and is now a global phenomenon spanning software companies across geographies.

The core argument of this talk: **the original PLG playbook (Slack, Dropbox, Trello, Evernote) is now dated.** Every company has copied it, and you need a new playbook for 2026.

The talk is structured as a history lesson — comparing the **2018 state of PLG** to the **2024/2025 state** — using real product screenshots and examples across seven strategic layers.

---

## What Product-Led Growth Actually Is

> "Product-led growth is a method of organizing and building a company. It's about putting the product first to acquire customers, to convert them, to engage them, retain them, and monetize them."
> — Blake Bartlett's original definition

**Common misconception #1:** PLG equals self-serve. In many companies, "PLG revenue" refers only to the self-serve motion, but true PLG is a company-wide philosophy, not just a sales channel.

PLG has two elements:
1. **Strategy** — Leadership alignment, cross-functional buy-in (CEO, VP Sales, VP Marketing). Without this, the PLG motion is doomed.
2. **Tactics** — The specific product and marketing actions across each layer of the funnel.

**The people problem:** PLG requires discovery-driven PMs, not project-manager/order-taker PMs. Rapid experimentation is the engine of PLG. You cannot predetermine a year in advance what to build — you learn, then iterate month to month. This demands tolerance for ambiguity from Sales and Marketing teams.

## The Seven-Layer PLG Framework

Developed collaboratively by Ash and Jared Herman through deep dives on companies including Miro, Canva, and others (5,000–10,000-word research pieces each).

| Layer | What It Is |
|---|---|
| 1 | Go-to-Market (Acquisition) |
| 2 | Information for Decision |
| 3 | Free-to-Paid Conversion |
| 4 | Activation |
| 5 | Retention |
| 6 | Monetization |
| 7 | Expansion |

**How to prioritize:** Do NOT tackle all layers simultaneously. Find where you are most broken and focus on that layer plus one or two layers below it. Going deeper without fixing upstream layers means insufficient volume for meaningful experiments.

## Layer 1: Go-to-Market (Acquisition)

**Two key product surface areas:**
- Your **marketing channels**
- Your **website**

### 2018 Benchmark: Slack
- Grew from 0 to **8 million daily active users** in four years
- Used **traditional marketing channels**: banner ads, buses, billboards
- Campaign positioning: Slack vs. email ("We're helping you get less email") — fighting the incumbent
- Website: Extremely simple, single-screen. Headline: *"Where work happens."*
- CTA: Drop in email → magic link → instantly inside a workspace (no password setup)
- Featured logos: Airbnb and other aspirational brands
- Emphasized: "Try it for free"

### 2024 Benchmark: Canva
- **170 million monthly active users**
- **$2 billion+ ARR**
- Growing at **~50% per year** (private company)
- Strategy: Does NOT spend on TV ads or traditional channels
- Uses **product-led SEO**: Creates top-ranking Google pages for high-volume search terms (e.g., "build an Instagram post")
- Landing: One click to a free tool, **no login, no credit card required**
- Acquires millions of users monthly from people who may have never heard of Canva
- Website: Long, personalized. Asks: Are you a workplace? An educator? A creator? Routes users to Free, Pro, Teams, or Enterprise plans
- Showcases: template gallery, AI features, client logos, multiple product demo slots
- Positioning: Not against an incumbent (Adobe) — focused entirely on the user's needs and Canva's product

### Key Evolution: Layer 1
| Dimension | 2018 (Slack) | 2024 (Canva) |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing channels | Traditional (brand ads, OOH) | Product-led SEO |
| Positioning | Against the incumbent (email) | User-focused, job-to-be-done |
| Website | Static, minimal | Dynamic, personalized journeys |
| Acquisition friction | Email → magic link | Zero friction (no login required for tool use) |

**Timeless principles that remain constant:** Direct users immediately into the product; emphasize free.

## Layer 2: Information for Decision

The goal: Give prospects the information they need to decide to try (and eventually pay for) your product.

**Two key product surface areas:**
- Your **pricing page** (the second most visited page on any SaaS website, without exception)
- **Lower-funnel marketing channels**: case studies, template galleries, referral programs

### 2018 Benchmark: Dropbox
- Named "king of freemium" by Thomas Tongas at IPO (2018)
- Pricing page: Tabs for "Individuals" and "Teams" (some personalization)
- Heavy use of the **feature comparison table** — rows of checkmarks
- Problem: As a user, it's unclear which bucket you belong to; lacks visual clarity
- Lower-funnel marketing: **Referral program** — the canonical example still cited today
  - Invite with Gmail CTA, share on Facebook
  - Tied to value metric: storage space (smart)
  - Very user-focused, not account-focused
  - Free storage reward mechanic

### 2024 Benchmark: Notion
- Grew from **0 to 30 million users** in the same timeframe Dropbox's stock went flat post-IPO
- Now **over $500 million ARR** — larger than Dropbox in many respects
- Pricing page: Clean, above-the-fold, simple visual boxes for each plan (Free, Plus, Business, Enterprise, AI)
- No long comparison table; product team makes deliberate choices about which features to highlight per plan
- Visual segmentation is instant — you immediately know which plan fits you
- Lower-funnel marketing: **Template galleries**
  - Dynamic, use-case-based (not transactional)
  - Drives account-level adoption — entire teams adopt a template together, not just individual users
  - Same strategy used by Miro, Canva, Figma

### Key Evolution: Layer 2
| Dimension | 2018 (Dropbox) | 2024 (Notion) |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing page | Static table with checkmarks | Clean visual segmentation, bold editorial choices |
| Lower-funnel channel | Referral program (user-focused) | Template gallery (account-focused) |
| Goal | Transactional incentive | Use-case clarity and team-level adoption |

## Layer 3: Free-to-Paid Conversion

Described as one of the most **neglected layers** in PLG literature, but one of the highest-leverage areas for immediate ARR impact. "I guarantee you if you just run a few experiments you'll see huge results."

**Two key product surface areas:**
- **Free plan configuration**: Freemium, free trial, reverse trial, or combinations
- **Feature/billing gates**: Paywalls that appear at usage limits or premium feature access points

### Free Plan Configuration Models (Kyle Pyers Data — annual survey of 2,000+ SaaS companies)

| Model | Free Audience Size | Paid Conversion Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Free Trial only | None (no free audience) | Equal |
| Freemium only | Largest | Equal |
| Trial + Freemium | Slightly smaller than freemium | Equal |
| Reverse Trial | Smaller free audience | **Highest paid conversion** |

Key insight: **Conversion rates are roughly equal across models** — but free audience size and expansion potential differ significantly. Choose based on your growth strategy.

### 2018 Benchmark: Dropbox
- Freemium model (2 GB free) + free trials for pro plan
- Does NOT require credit card for freemium; DOES require it for free trial (capture people who forget to cancel)
- Billing gate: "It looks as though you're out of space. It costs 2 GB." — functional but generic

### 2024 Benchmarks: Adio + Canva

**Adio** (Series B, $33M raised, targeting Salesforce/HubSpot/PipeDrive market, growing **100%+ per year**):
- Introduced **reverse trial in September 2023** after reading content from Ash, Elena Vera, and Kyle Pyers
- Result: **Nearly doubled their free-to-paid conversion rate**
- Flow: User selects "free plan" → greeted with "Welcome to Adio Pro — you have 14 days of Pro, no credit card required"
- Notable: **Shows enterprise pricing** on pricing page (rare among SaaS companies)

**Canva:**
- Has **16 contextual billing gates** throughout the product
- Each gate shows you exactly what you'll get on the paid plan — done for you, in context
- Examples:
  - Image resize gate: Canva resizes it for you, then shows you the resized versions and says "upgrade to Pro to access these"
  - Watermark gate: Shares artifact with watermark; offers option to pay $7 one-time fee OR upgrade to Pro
- These gates feel like product value demonstrations, not blockers

**Reverse trial real-world example:** Tesla gave all users a free month of Full Self-Driving in March (year unspecified). Generated **$160 million in ARR**. Spotify has also adopted the reverse trial model.

### Key Evolution: Layer 3
| Dimension | 2018 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Free configuration | Freemium or trial | Reverse trial (premium access first, then drops to free) |
| Billing gates | Generic limit messages | Contextual, value-demonstrating gates (show what you get) |

## Layer 4: Activation

The "classical growth team" territory. Many companies have dedicated activation squads.

**Key insight:** Activation is not just about onboarding — it extends to **in-product habit formation**.

Mental model: Don't just optimize for "activation to setup" or "activation to aha moment" — optimize for **activation to habit**.

**Two key product surface areas:**
- **Onboarding flow** (including in-product checklists)
- **In-app homepage**

### 2018 Benchmark: Box
- Made a major PLG shift in 2018, hired top PLG PMs and consultants
- Implemented **step-based onboarding**: one forced next action at a time
- Used **gamification** (à la Duolingo): task completion rewards, gifted rewards, streak mechanics
- Problem: One-size-fits-all, forced linear flow

### 2024 Benchmark: Calendly
- **$270 million ARR**, growing at **40% per year**
- Onboarding: Immediately personalizes — "Are you a salesperson?" → connect your CRM → record meetings. "Are you booking casual meetings?" → skip CRM, go straight to setup
- Drives immediately to the **aha moment** for each persona
- In-app homepage: Personalized experience for continued activation post-onboarding
  - Includes demo video
  - Support contact option
  - Sales contact option
  - Gamification elements borrowed from Box's best practices

### Key Evolution: Layer 4
| Dimension | 2018 (Box) | 2024 (Calendly) |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding style | Forced step-based, one-size-fits-all | Forked (3–4 persona paths), self-paced |
| Activation scope | Onboarding only | Onboarding + in-app homepage as ongoing activation surface |
| Goal | Activation to aha | Activation to habit |

**Actionable advice:** If you have only one onboarding flow today, create forks for 3–4 user types. And invest in your in-app homepage as an ongoing activation surface — add demo video, support access, and sales contact.

## Layer 5: Retention

Retention directly drives valuation. Snowflake at **150%+ NRR** was trading at **30x next year's sales**. Wall Street and VCs price companies heavily on retention quality.

**Two key product surface areas:**
- **Habit loops** you're driving
- **Product updates and ongoing value delivery**

### 2018 Benchmark: Evernote
- Famous for the **"smile chart"** — a rare and exceptional retention curve
- Standard retention charts decline continuously
- Evernote's chart: drops initially, then curves back upward — returning to near-month-1 retention levels at the **3-year mark**
- Built on: cross-device syncing (revolutionary when Apple Notes was weak), strong note organization, "second brain" concept (Tiago Forte's *Building a Second Brain* popularized Evernote as the tool)
- **Failure point:** Did not expand into team-wide collaboration
- Result today: App downloads dropped from **10 million/year to nearly zero**

### 2024 Benchmark: Figma
- In 2018: **$4 million ARR**
- At time of talk: **$700 million ARR**, growing at **35% per year**
- Took all market share from Sketch
- Retention drivers:
  - **Real-time collaboration** ("those infamous moving cursors") — expanded to team use case
  - Deep integrations (Figma files embedded in Slack and Notion look beautiful and are easily clickable)
  - Continuous feature rollouts: Slides, Dev Mode, FigJam (for brainstorming — used by marketers, sales, customer success, not just designers)
- Infiltration model: Starts with designers and engineers → PMs comment on files → researchers join → marketers use FigJam → company-wide retention

> "Company-wide retention is ultimately the name of the game."

### Key Evolution: Layer 5
| Dimension | 2018 (Evernote) | 2024 (Figma) |
|---|---|---|
| Retention unit | Individual user | Team → Department → Company |
| Habit loop | Personal notes + cross-device | Real-time collaboration + integrations |
| Expansion surface | None (solo use case) | FigJam, Dev Mode, Slides (new departments, new personas) |

## Layer 6: Monetization

**Two key product surface areas:**
- **Pricing tiers** (structure and clarity)
- **Pricing model** (seat-based vs. usage-based vs. hybrid)

**Industry data (Kyle Pyers):** Nearly **50% of SaaS companies** now have usage-based or hybrid pricing in addition to seat-based pricing, up from near zero six years ago.

### 2018 Benchmark: Trello
- Acquired by Atlassian in 2017 for a large sum; sparked endless case studies
- Three simple tiers with freemium entry
- **Power-Ups**: A upsell feature mechanic that was innovative and still effective
- Organic growth, but limited cross-team or cross-department strategy
- No usage-based pricing component

### 2024 Benchmark: HubSpot
- **Stock performance:** Described as a "17-bagger" — one of the best-performing SaaS stocks
- Strategy: Built products for **every team in the organization**
  - Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, Service Hub, Operations Hub, and more
  - In sales tech: recently added **data enrichment** (a space they hadn't entered before)
  - Free CRM as the Trojan horse to enter the sales team, then layered enrichment and additional products
- Enterprise features: Rule-based access control, SSO, and other "boring but necessary" enterprise functionality — allowed expansion from SMB to mid-market and enterprise
- Pricing page: Right-side navigation shows every product line for every team

### 2024 Benchmark: Apollo.io (speaker's former company)
- Became a **unicorn ($1 billion+ ARR)** while speaker was VP of Product
- Surpassed **40,000 paying accounts**
- Had all standard PLG elements: freemium, free trial, tiered pricing
- Key differentiator: **Usage-based pricing slider** at the bottom of the pricing page
  - Apollo is one of the best contact databases in the world after LinkedIn
  - Companies buy seats (top of pricing page) AND buy additional contacts via slider (e.g., 100,000 additional contacts)
  - Further granularity: specific types of contacts (mobile phone numbers vs. emails) at different price points
  - **This usage vector drove 70–80% of expansion revenue**
  - Customers bought additional contacts at month 1, month 6, month 11, and at renewal

### Key Evolution: Layer 6
| Dimension | 2018 (Trello) | 2024 (HubSpot / Apollo) |
|---|---|---|
| Tier focus | User-level feature gating | Account + team + department-level |
| Pricing model | Seat-based only | Hybrid: seat + usage-based |
| Expansion mechanism | Organic, passive | Structured cross-department product lines + usage dials |
| Enterprise strategy | Minimal | Deep (SSO, RBAC, enterprise compliance) |

## Layer 7: Expansion

Described as the **most neglected layer** (alongside free-to-paid conversion). Strong expansion reduces dependence on expensive outbound sales motions.

**Two key product surface areas:**
- **Additional features** (e.g., AI add-ons, new capability modules)
- **Cross-org expansion** (entering new departments within the same account)

### 2018 Benchmark: SurveyMonkey
- 2018 IPO; subsequent history: disastrous rebrand to **Momentive**, then acquired by PE firm STG, then rebranded back to SurveyMonkey
- Had freemium with tier upgrades and feature gating
- **Missing:** Team/seat concept was underdeveloped, no usage-based or hybrid pricing
- Limited customization of plans
- No clear cross-department strategy

### 2024 Benchmark: Apollo.io
- Usage-based expansion drove **70–80% of expansion ARR** (as noted in Layer 6)
- Expansion happened at multiple points in the customer lifecycle, not just at signup

### Key Evolution: Layer 7
| Dimension | 2018 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Expansion focus | Individual users, tier upgrades | Cross-department, usage-based, multi-product |
| Expansion trigger | Manual/sales-driven | Product-embedded (usage gates, contextual upsells) |
| Revenue leverage | Low | High — reduces need for outbound sales |

## The Three Meta-Vectors of PLG Evolution

Summing up the evolution across all seven layers into three overarching trends:

### 1. Personalization
Not a buzzword — a concrete, implementable strategy across all seven layers:
- Personalized landing pages by user type (Canva)
- Personalized pricing page segmentation (Notion)
- Personalized onboarding forks (Calendly)
- Personalized in-app homepage (Calendly)
- Contextual billing gates that show value per user action (Canva)

### 2. Whole-Org Infiltration
- Don't build retention and expansion for individual users — build for **teams, departments, and companies**
- Examples: Figma's FigJam reaching marketers and sales, HubSpot's per-department product lines, Apollo's contact enrichment purchased by whole sales orgs

### 3. Evolved Freemium
- Freemium is no longer just "add a free tier and call it PLG"
- The new model: **Reverse trial** — give users a taste of premium functionality upfront
  - Tesla Full Self-Driving free month → **$160 million in ARR**
  - Adio reverse trial → nearly **doubled free-to-paid conversion**
  - Spotify has also adopted reverse trial
- Combine with contextual billing gates that demonstrate value rather than block it

## Actionable Takeaways by Layer

**Layer 1 — Go-to-Market:**
- Invest in product-led SEO — create free tools that rank for high-volume job-to-be-done search terms
- Build personalized landing pages for different user types (B2C creator, B2B team, enterprise buyer)
- Remove friction from product entry (no login required for initial tool use if possible)

**Layer 2 — Information for Decision:**
- Redesign your pricing page: ditch the feature comparison table; use clean visual segmentation so users can self-identify their plan instantly
- Make editorial choices about which 3–4 features to highlight per plan — you can't show everything
- Replace or supplement your referral program with a template gallery that drives account-level adoption

**Layer 3 — Free-to-Paid Conversion:**
- Test a **reverse trial**: Give new free signups access to Pro features for 14 days, no credit card required
- Build **contextual billing gates**: When a user tries a premium feature, show them the completed output and gate access to it — don't just show an error message
- Consider showing enterprise pricing on your pricing page (rare but builds trust)

**Layer 4 — Activation:**
- Break your single onboarding flow into **3–4 persona forks** — stop forcing everyone through the same path
- Invest in your **in-app homepage** as a persistent activation surface: add demo video, support access, sales contact, and progress indicators
- Optimize for **activation to habit**, not just activation to aha moment

**Layer 5 — Retention:**
- Build retention at the **account and team level**, not just the individual user level
- Drive real-time collaboration features that pull in other departments
- Invest in integrations that make your product embed naturally in other tools (e.g., Figma previews in Slack/Notion)
- Release a continuous cadence of features that expand use cases to new personas within the account

**Layer 6 — Monetization:**
- Add a **usage-based pricing component** alongside seat-based pricing (dial, slider, or credit system)
- Build out **per-department product lines** so each team in a company has a reason to pay
- Invest in enterprise features (RBAC, SSO, audit logs) to unlock mid-market and enterprise expansion
- Study Apollo's model: usage-based expansion can dwarf seat-based expansion

**Layer 7 — Expansion:**
- Identify adjacent departments that could use your product and build features or products for them
- Build expansion into the product motion itself — don't rely solely on sales to drive upsell
- Track expansion at the account level, not just the user level

## Framework Summary (One-Page Mental Model)

```
PLG = Strategy + Tactics + People

Strategy = 7 Layers (top to bottom):
  1. Go-to-Market       → Surface: Marketing + Website
  2. Info for Decision  → Surface: Pricing Page + Lower-Funnel Channels
  3. Free→Paid Conv.    → Surface: Free Config (reverse trial) + Billing Gates
  4. Activation         → Surface: Onboarding Flow + In-App Homepage
  5. Retention          → Surface: Habit Loops + Feature Updates
  6. Monetization       → Surface: Pricing Tiers + Pricing Model (usage/hybrid)
  7. Expansion          → Surface: Additional Features + Cross-Org Growth

Focus rule: Fix the layer where you're most broken, plus 1–2 layers below it.
Don't skip layers — you won't have the volume to run experiments further down.

3 Meta-Vectors of Evolution:
  - Personalization (across all 7 layers)
  - Whole-org infiltration (team → department → company retention)
  - Evolved freemium (reverse trial > traditional freemium/trial)
```
